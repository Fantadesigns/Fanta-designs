About the Project

The University of Nigeria, Nsukka had no digital presence for its library. Students had to physically visit to access materials — many dating back to the 1960s and rapidly deteriorating. Over 90 million pages were repaired, scanned, and digitised. The challenge was to design a mobile experience modern enough for today's students yet respectful of decades of academic history.

Outcomes

The app is in its final stages ahead of a university-wide mandatory rollout. With 60,000+ students, lecturers, and staff required to sign up, the project is targeting 50% active adoption within the first two months of launch — making it one of the largest ed-tech launches from a Nigerian university.

Process

User research

Interviewed students, lecturers, and library staff to map real access patterns — discovering offline access and role-based views as the two most critical design requirements.

High-fidelity design

Designed the full app in Figma — covering onboarding, home, search, reading experience, bookmarks, and a separate lecturer view with course-linked reading lists.

Design system

Built a full component library with colour tokens, typography, icons, and reusable UI patterns to ensure visual consistency across hundreds of screens.

Prototyping & Testing

Ran usability sessions with students to validate navigation patterns, reading experience, and onboarding flow — iterating on feedback before final handoff to engineering.
